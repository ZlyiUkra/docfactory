Search books and papers about systematic trading: how to design a trading system with rules, measure its risk and reward,
size positions, test it on history without fooling yourself, and trade currencies. The corpus holds ten books and four
papers: Tharp, Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom (2nd ed.: risk in units of R, R-multiples and expectancy, setups,
entries, stops and profit-taking exits, position sizing models); Carver, Leveraged Trading (FX, CFDs, margin, costs, risk
and position size for a private trader); Aronson, Evidence-Based Technical Analysis (data-mining bias, statistical tests
of trading rules); Pardo, The Evaluation and Optimization of Trading Strategies (2nd ed.: historical simulation,
optimization, walk-forward analysis, overfitting); Chan, Algorithmic Trading (backtesting pitfalls, mean reversion,
including currencies, momentum, risk management) and Quantitative Trading (2nd ed.: backtesting, execution, money and
risk management, the Kelly formula); Hilpisch, Python for Algorithmic Trading (vectorized and event-based backtesting,
predicting market movements with machine learning, CFD trading with Oanda, FX trading with FXCM, automation) and
Artificial Intelligence in Finance (neural networks and reinforcement learning for finance, statistical inefficiencies,
algorithmic trading, risk management); Vishwas and Patel, Hands-on Time Series Analysis with Python; Sperandeo, Methods of
a Wall Street Master (trends, the 2B change of trend, measuring risk; recognised from a scan); and the papers of Bailey and
López de Prado on the probability of backtest overfitting, the deflated Sharpe ratio and the Sharpe ratio efficient
frontier.

Use this tool for: what an R-multiple and expectancy are, how a fixed stop-loss and profit target interact with the win
rate, which position sizing model to use, how to set up a walk-forward test, how to tell a real edge from an overfit
backtest, the Kelly formula, what costs and leverage do to a retail FX account, and how machine learning or reinforcement
learning is applied to trading in these books. Write the query in English with the term the books use, such as
`R-multiple`, `expectancy`, `percent risk model`, `walk-forward`, `deflated Sharpe ratio` or `Kelly`.

Do not use it for library or platform documentation (MetaTrader, indicator libraries, backtesting libraries, LightGBM,
PyTorch, ONNX belong to other servers) or for the theory of decision trees and reinforcement learning in general (the
tree-models and reinforcement-learning servers hold those books). Returns the matching excerpts with the book or paper,
the source address and the fetch date. The books are not versioned.

Every excerpt carries an `id`; pass the `id` of an excerpt to `read_section` to get the whole thing.

The `search` field of the result says how the excerpts were found: "words" means the words of the query had to appear
in the text, so a query phrased in other words than the documentation uses may miss; "meaning+words" means a second,
meaning-based index answered as well, and a paraphrase had a chance.
