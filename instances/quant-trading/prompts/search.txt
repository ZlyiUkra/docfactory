Search books and papers about systematic trading: how to design a trading system with rules, measure its risk and reward,
size positions, test it on history without fooling yourself, and trade currencies. It also covers the Wyckoff method. The
corpus holds seventeen books, three short guides, one translated article series and four papers: Tharp, Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom (2nd ed.: risk in units of R, R-multiples and expectancy, setups,
entries, stops and profit-taking exits, position sizing models); Carver, Leveraged Trading (FX, CFDs, margin, costs, risk
and position size for a private trader); Aronson, Evidence-Based Technical Analysis (data-mining bias, statistical tests
of trading rules); Pardo, The Evaluation and Optimization of Trading Strategies (2nd ed.: historical simulation,
optimization, walk-forward analysis, overfitting); Chan, Algorithmic Trading (backtesting pitfalls, mean reversion,
including currencies, momentum, risk management) and Quantitative Trading (2nd ed.: backtesting, execution, money and
risk management, the Kelly formula); Hilpisch, Python for Algorithmic Trading (vectorized and event-based backtesting,
predicting market movements with machine learning, CFD trading with Oanda, FX trading with FXCM, automation) and
Artificial Intelligence in Finance (neural networks and reinforcement learning for finance, statistical inefficiencies,
algorithmic trading, risk management); Vishwas and Patel, Hands-on Time Series Analysis with Python; Sperandeo, Methods of
a Wall Street Master (trends, the 1-2-3 and 2B change of trend, measuring risk; the text was recognised from a scan long ago
and still has some errors) and Trader Vic on Commodities (the 2B rule, trend following, the S&P DTI index); LumiTraders,
Unlocking Success in ICT 2022 Mentorship, with two short guides on ICT and Smart Money (liquidity sweeps, fair value gaps,
order blocks, displacement, sessions and kill zones, Power of 3: the authors' explanations, not tested rules); Frost and
Prechter, Elliott Wave Principle (wave counts, guidelines of wave formation, Fibonacci ratios); the Wyckoff method:
Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth (accumulation and distribution structures, the three laws, the events PS, SC,
AR, ST, spring, upthrust, SOS, SOW, LPS, phases A to E, entries in phases C, D and E, position management), Wyckoff Analytics,
The Wyckoff Method: A Tutorial (events, phases, the nine buying and selling tests, point and figure counts), Weis, Trades About
to Happen (springs, upthrusts, absorption, bar charts with volume, Weis waves), Hutson, The Wyckoff Method of Trading Stocks (a
Russian translation of his article series), and two books by Wyckoff himself from 1910 and 1926, Studies in Tape Reading and How
I Trade and Invest in Stocks and Bonds (scans, their recognised text has errors); and the papers of Bailey and López de Prado on
the probability of backtest overfitting, the deflated Sharpe ratio and the Sharpe ratio efficient frontier.

Use this tool for: what an R-multiple and expectancy are, how a fixed stop-loss and profit target interact with the win
rate, which position sizing model to use, how to set up a walk-forward test, how to tell a real edge from an overfit
backtest, the Kelly formula, what costs and leverage do to a retail FX account, and how machine learning or reinforcement
learning is applied to trading in these books, what the 2B false breakout,
the ICT concepts and Elliott waves claim and how they are defined, and how the Wyckoff method reads accumulation and
distribution: the phases, the spring and the upthrust, the sign of strength, effort versus result. Write the query in English
with the term the books use, such as `R-multiple`, `expectancy`, `percent risk model`, `walk-forward`, `deflated Sharpe ratio`,
`Kelly`, `2B rule`, `fair value gap`, `depth of corrective waves`, `spring`, `upthrust after distribution` or `effort and
result`. Hutson's text is Russian: a query in Russian (`трамплин`, `накопление`, `кульминация продаж`) searches it by words
only, and Ukrainian words match only where they are spelled as in Russian.

Do not use it for library or platform documentation (MetaTrader, indicator libraries, backtesting libraries, LightGBM,
PyTorch, ONNX belong to other servers) or for the theory of decision trees and reinforcement learning in general (the
tree-models and reinforcement-learning servers hold those books). Returns the matching excerpts with the book or paper,
the source address and the fetch date. The books are not versioned.

Every excerpt carries an `id`; pass the `id` of an excerpt to `read_section` to get the whole thing.

The `search` field of the result says how the excerpts were found: "words" means the words of the query had to appear
in the text, so a query phrased in other words than the documentation uses may miss; "meaning+words" means a second,
meaning-based index answered as well, and a paraphrase had a chance.
