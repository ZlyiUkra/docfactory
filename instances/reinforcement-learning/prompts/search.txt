Search books, courses and papers about reinforcement learning and bandits. The corpus holds chapters of books: Sutton and Barto,
Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (2nd ed.); Lapan, Deep Reinforcement Learning Hands-On (3rd ed.); Graesser and Keng,
Foundations of Deep Reinforcement Learning; Morales, Grokking Deep Reinforcement Learning; Szepesvári, Algorithms for Reinforcement
Learning; Lattimore and Szepesvári, Bandit Algorithms. It also holds courses: OpenAI Spinning Up in Deep RL, the Hugging Face Deep
Reinforcement Learning Course, the slides of David Silver's UCL course. And papers: DQN, Double DQN, Dueling DQN, Prioritized
Experience Replay, Rainbow, A3C, GAE, TRPO, PPO, DDPG, TD3, Soft Actor-Critic; offline reinforcement learning: the tutorial of
Levine et al., Conservative Q-Learning, Implicit Q-Learning, Decision Transformer; trading: Deep Reinforcement Learning for Trading
of Zhang, Zohren and Roberts, and FinRL.

Use this tool for: what a Markov decision process, a return, a value function and a policy are; the Bellman equation; Monte Carlo
and temporal-difference learning, SARSA and Q-learning; exploration against exploitation, epsilon-greedy, UCB and Thompson
sampling, regret, contextual and linear bandits; policy gradient, REINFORCE, actor-critic, advantage estimation; how DQN, PPO, SAC,
TD3 and their extensions work, which hyperparameters matter and why training is unstable; reward design; learning from a fixed
dataset without an environment (offline reinforcement learning); a trading environment, its state, actions and reward. Write the
query in English with the name of the method or with plain words for what is wanted.

Do not use this tool for the API of Gymnasium, Stable-Baselines3, d3rlpy, PyTorch or NumPy: other servers hold those references.
Returns the matching excerpts with the source address and the fetch date.

Every excerpt carries an `id`; pass the `id` of an excerpt to `read_section` to get the whole thing.

The `search` field of the result says how the excerpts were found: "words" means the words of the query had to appear
in the text, so a query phrased in other words than the documentation uses may miss; "meaning+words" means a second,
meaning-based index answered as well, and a paraphrase had a chance.
